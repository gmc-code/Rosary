================================
A short history of the Rosary
================================

The Rosary is a Catholic prayer that grew gradually over many centuries,
rather than being invented all at once. It joins two things: vocal prayer
(the Our Father, Hail Mary and Glory Be, counted on beads) and meditation on
the great events, or *mysteries*, in the lives of Jesus and Mary.


Praying the Psalms on beads
===========================

The roots of the Rosary go back to the monks of the early Church, who prayed
all 150 Psalms each week. Many ordinary people could not read, and could not
own a book, so they prayed 150 Our Fathers instead, often counting them on a
string of knots or beads. This is why the full Rosary was long known as
"Mary's Psalter".


The Hail Mary
=============

By the 12th and 13th centuries, devotion to Mary had grown, and people began
to repeat the angel Gabriel's greeting, "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is
with you" (Luke 1:28), joined to Elizabeth's words, "Blessed are you among
women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb" (Luke 1:42). The second half,
"Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners…", was added later and was
settled in its present form in 1568.

Tradition says that Mary gave the Rosary to St Dominic (1170–1221), founder
of the Dominicans, to help him preach. Historians believe the Rosary grew
over a long time rather than beginning with one person, but the Dominicans
did more than anyone to spread it, and they are still closely linked with it.


Adding the mysteries
====================

In the early 1400s, Dominic of Prussia, a Carthusian monk, added a short
thought about the life of Christ to each Hail Mary, so that people
meditated as they prayed. Later in the 1400s, Blessed Alan de la Roche, a
Dominican, grouped the prayers into decades, founded Rosary confraternities
and preached the Rosary across Europe.

The name comes from the Latin *rosarium*, a "rose garden" or "garland of
roses": each prayer is like a rose offered to Mary.


Lepanto and Our Lady of the Rosary
==================================

In 1569 Pope St Pius V, himself a Dominican, set out the Rosary in the form
that lasted for over four hundred years: fifteen decades, each with its
mystery, in three sets of Joyful, Sorrowful and Glorious Mysteries.

In 1571 he asked Christians across Europe to pray the Rosary for the fleet
facing the Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto, off the coast of Greece.
The victory on 7 October 1571 was put down to their prayers, and Pius V
began a feast of Our Lady of Victory. Two years later Pope Gregory XIII
renamed it Our Lady of the Rosary, and it is still kept on 7 October.


Recent centuries
================

In the late 1800s Pope Leo XIII wrote many letters on the Rosary and made
October the month of the Rosary. In 1917, at Fatima in Portugal, the three
shepherd children reported that Mary asked them to pray the Rosary every day
for peace; the short Fatima prayer said after each Glory Be ("O my Jesus,
forgive us our sins…") comes from there.

For centuries the Rosary had fifteen mysteries. In October 2002, in his
letter *Rosarium Virginis Mariae*, Pope St John Paul II added the five
Luminous Mysteries, or Mysteries of Light, on Jesus' public life, from his
Baptism to the Last Supper. This brought the total to twenty, and he
suggested praying them on Thursdays.


The Rosary today
================

The Rosary is still one of the best-loved Catholic prayers, prayed alone,
in families, in schools and in parishes. It can be prayed anywhere, needs
nothing but a string of beads (or ten fingers), and leads the person praying
through the whole story of the Gospel, from the Annunciation to the glory of
heaven.
