================================
A short history of the Rosary
================================

The Rosary is a Catholic prayer that grew gradually over many centuries,
rather than being invented all at once. It joins two things: vocal prayer
(the Our Father, Hail Mary and Glory Be, counted on beads) and meditation on
the great events, or *mysteries*, in the lives of Jesus and Mary. [#f1]_


Praying on beads
================

Christians have long used knotted cords, pebbles or beads to keep count of
repeated prayers. Monks of the early Church prayed all 150 Psalms, and by the
12th century many lay people prayed 150 Our Fathers instead, counting them on
strings of beads. This was well before the time of St Dominic. Not everyone
had a book or could read Latin, but the beads were, above all, a simple way
of keeping count. [#f1]_


The Hail Mary
=============

By the 12th century, devotion to Mary had grown, and people began to repeat
the angel Gabriel's greeting, "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you"
(Luke 1:28), joined to Elizabeth's words, "Blessed are you among women, and
blessed is the fruit of your womb" (Luke 1:42). A set of 150 Hail Marys came
to be called "Our Lady's Psalter", because the number matched the Psalms,
not because the Hail Marys are Psalms. The second half of the prayer, "Holy
Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners…", was added later and settled in
its present form in the Roman Breviary of 1568. [#f1]_


Adding the mysteries
====================

In the early 1400s, Dominic of Prussia, a Carthusian monk near Trier, wrote
fifty short phrases about the lives of Jesus and Mary, one to be added after
the name of Jesus in each of fifty Hail Marys. People could now meditate on
the Gospel as they prayed. Others soon expanded his idea to the full 150.
[#f1]_

Later in the 1400s, Blessed Alan de la Roche, a Dominican preacher, spread
this "Psalter of Jesus and Mary" across northern Europe. He founded a Rosary
confraternity at Douai in about 1470, and confraternities soon spread from
Cologne throughout Europe, with members promising to pray the Rosary
regularly. [#f1]_

Tradition says that Mary gave the Rosary to St Dominic (1170–1221), founder
of the Dominicans, to help him preach, and many popes have repeated this.
[#f4]_ Much of the story comes from Alan de la Roche's preaching, and
historians note that the earliest accounts of Dominic's life do not mention
the Rosary. Either way, the Dominicans did more than anyone to spread the
Rosary, and they are still closely linked with it. [#f1]_

The name comes from the Latin *rosarium*, a "rose garden" or "garland of
roses": each prayer is like a rose offered to Mary.


Pius V and the fifteen mysteries
================================

In 1569 Pope St Pius V, himself a Dominican, issued the bull *Consueverunt
Romani Pontifices*, which approved the Rosary in the form that lasted for
over four hundred years: fifteen decades, each of ten Hail Marys introduced
by an Our Father, with a mystery for each decade, in three sets of Joyful,
Sorrowful and Glorious Mysteries. [#f1]_


Lepanto and Our Lady of the Rosary
==================================

In 1571 a fleet of the Holy League, an alliance of Catholic states, met the
Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto, off the coast of Greece. Pius V asked
Christians to pray the Rosary for the fleet, and Rosary confraternities held
processions in Rome. The victory on 7 October 1571 was put down to their
prayers, and Pius V ordered a yearly commemoration of Our Lady of Victory.
[#f2]_

In 1573 Pope Gregory XIII renamed it the feast of the Holy Rosary, but
allowed it only in churches that had an altar of the Rosary. In 1716, after
another victory over Ottoman forces at Petrovaradin in Hungary, Pope Clement
XI extended the feast to the whole Church. For many years it was kept on the
first Sunday of October; in 1913 Pope St Pius X fixed it on 7 October, where
it remains as Our Lady of the Rosary. [#f2]_


The month of the Rosary
=======================

Pope Leo XIII (1878–1903) wrote twelve encyclicals on the Rosary, more than
on any other subject. In the first, *Supremi Apostolatus Officio* (1883), he
asked that the whole month of October be consecrated to the Queen of the
Rosary, and he added the title "Queen of the Most Holy Rosary" to the Litany
of Loreto. October has been the month of the Rosary ever since. In his last,
*Diuturni Temporis* (1898), he looked back on all he had written. [#f3]_
[#f5]_


Fatima
======

In 1917, at Fatima in Portugal, three shepherd children, Lucia dos Santos and
her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, reported that Mary appeared to them
six times, on the 13th of each month from May to October. Throughout, she
asked them to pray the Rosary every day for peace and the end of the war, and
in the last appearance, on 13 October, she called herself "the Lady of the
Rosary". [#f8]_ [#f9]_ The short Fatima prayer said after each Glory Be
("O my Jesus, forgive us our sins…") comes from the July appearance. [#f9]_

On 13 October 1930, after a long investigation, the Bishop of Leiria
declared the visions of the children "worthy of credit" and officially
permitted devotion to Our Lady of Fatima. [#f10]_


The Luminous Mysteries
======================

In 1974 Pope St Paul VI, in *Marialis Cultus*, described the Rosary as a
"Gospel prayer" centred on Christ, and encouraged families to pray it
together. [#f6]_

For more than four centuries the Rosary had fifteen mysteries. On 16 October
2002, in his apostolic letter *Rosarium Virginis Mariae*, Pope St John Paul
II added the five Luminous Mysteries, or Mysteries of Light, on Jesus'
public life, from his Baptism in the Jordan to the institution of the
Eucharist at the Last Supper. This brought the total to twenty, in the four
sets of five prayed on this site. [#f7]_ He suggested praying them on
Thursdays, [#f11]_ and declared October 2002 to October 2003 the Year of the
Rosary. [#f12]_


The Rosary today
================

The Rosary is still one of the best-loved Catholic prayers, prayed alone,
in families, in schools and in parishes. It can be prayed anywhere, needs
nothing but a string of beads (or ten fingers), and leads the person praying
through the whole story of the Gospel, from the Annunciation to the glory of
heaven.
