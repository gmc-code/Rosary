================================
A short history of the Rosary
================================

The Rosary was not invented all at once. It grew slowly, over hundreds of
years, out of two older habits: repeating short prayers while counting them
on beads, and thinking quietly about events in the lives of Jesus and Mary.
Today these events are called the *mysteries* of the Rosary. [#ce-rosary]_


At a glance
===========

- **1100s** – Christians pray 150 Our Fathers or Hail Marys, counted on beads.
- **Early 1400s** – Dominic of Prussia links a thought about Jesus to each
  Hail Mary.
- **About 1470** – Alan de la Roche starts the first Rosary groups.
- **1569** – Pope Pius V sets the Rosary at fifteen mysteries.
- **1571** – Victory at the Battle of Lepanto; a feast of the Rosary follows.
- **1883** – Pope Leo XIII makes October the month of the Rosary.
- **1917** – At Fatima, Mary is reported to ask for the Rosary every day.
- **2002** – Pope John Paul II adds the five Luminous Mysteries.


Counting prayers on beads
=========================

People have long used knotted cords, pebbles or beads to keep count of
prayers they repeat. In the early Church, monks prayed all 150 Psalms of the
Bible. By the 1100s, many ordinary Christians prayed 150 Our Fathers instead,
counting them on a string of beads. [#ce-rosary]_


The Hail Mary
=============

Around the same time, people began to repeat a short prayer to Mary made
from two greetings in Luke's Gospel:

- the angel Gabriel's words, "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you"
  (Luke 1:28), and
- Elizabeth's words, "Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit
  of your womb" (Luke 1:42).

A set of 150 Hail Marys became known as "Our Lady's Psalter", because it
matched the number of Psalms. The second half of the prayer, "Holy Mary,
Mother of God, pray for us sinners…", came later. It was fixed in its present
form in 1568, in the Church's official prayer book, the Roman Breviary.
[#ce-rosary]_


Adding the mysteries
====================

In the early 1400s, Dominic of Prussia, a Carthusian monk living near Trier
in Germany, had a new idea. He wrote fifty short phrases about the lives of
Jesus and Mary, one to be added to each of fifty Hail Marys. Now people could
think about the Gospel story as they prayed. Others soon stretched his idea
to all 150. [#ce-rosary]_

Later that century, Blessed Alan de la Roche, a Dominican friar, preached
this way of praying across northern Europe. In about 1470 he started a
Rosary *confraternity* at Douai: a group whose members promised to pray the
Rosary regularly. Groups like this soon spread from Cologne all over Europe.
[#ce-rosary]_

The name comes from the Latin *rosarium*, meaning "rose garden" or "garland
of roses". Each prayer is like a rose offered to Mary.


St Dominic and the Rosary
=========================

A well-loved tradition says that Mary gave the Rosary to St Dominic
(1170–1221), who founded the Dominicans, to help him preach. Many popes have
repeated this story. [#octobri]_

Historians point out that much of the story comes from Alan de la Roche's
preaching, and that the earliest accounts of Dominic's life do not mention
the Rosary. Either way, the Dominicans did more than anyone to spread the
Rosary, and they are still closely linked with it. [#ce-rosary]_


Fifteen mysteries
=================

In 1569 Pope St Pius V, himself a Dominican, issued an official letter
(a *bull*) that set out the Rosary in the form it kept for more than four
hundred years:

- fifteen *decades*, each one Our Father followed by ten Hail Marys;
- one mystery to think about during each decade;
- three sets of five: the Joyful, Sorrowful and Glorious Mysteries.
  [#ce-rosary]_


Lepanto and the feast of the Rosary
===================================

On 7 October 1571, the fleet of the Holy League, an alliance of Catholic
states, defeated the Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto, off the coast of
Greece. Pius V had asked Christians to pray the Rosary for the fleet, and
Rosary groups held processions in Rome that day. The victory was put down to
their prayers, and Pius V set up a yearly feast of Our Lady of Victory.
[#ce-feast]_

The feast then grew step by step:

- **1573** – Pope Gregory XIII renamed it the feast of the Holy Rosary, for
  churches with a Rosary altar.
- **1716** – After another victory over Ottoman forces, at Petrovaradin in
  Hungary, Pope Clement XI extended it to the whole Church. [#ce-feast]_
- **1913** – Pope St Pius X moved it from the first Sunday of October to
  7 October, where it is still kept as Our Lady of the Rosary. [#nlm]_


October, the month of the Rosary
================================

Pope Leo XIII (pope 1878–1903) wrote twelve *encyclicals*, or letters to the
whole Church, on the Rosary. In the first, in 1883, he asked that the whole
month of October be given to the Rosary. October has been the month of the
Rosary ever since. [#supremi]_ He also added the title "Queen of the Most
Holy Rosary" to the Litany of Loreto. [#ce-feast]_ His last Rosary letter,
in 1898, looked back on all he had written. [#diuturni]_


Fatima
======

In 1917, at Fatima in Portugal, three shepherd children, Lucia dos Santos and
her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, said that Mary appeared to them six
times, on the 13th of each month from May to October. Again and again she
asked them to pray the Rosary every day, for peace and an end to the war. At
the last appearance, on 13 October, she called herself "the Lady of the
Rosary". [#fatima-cdf]_ [#lucia]_

The short Fatima prayer said after each Glory Be, "O my Jesus, forgive us our sins,
save us from the fires of hell, lead all souls to heaven,
especially those in most need of thy mercy.", comes from the July appearance. [#lucia]_

In 1930, after a long investigation, the local bishop declared the children's
visions "worthy of credit" and approved devotion to Our Lady of Fatima.
[#leiria]_


Twenty mysteries
================

In 1974 Pope St Paul VI called the Rosary a "Gospel prayer" centred on
Christ, and urged families to pray it together. [#marialis]_

For over four hundred years the Rosary had fifteen mysteries. Then, in
October 2002, Pope St John Paul II added five more: the Luminous Mysteries,
or Mysteries of Light. They cover Jesus' public life, from his Baptism in the
Jordan to the first Eucharist at the Last Supper. This brought the total to
twenty, in the four sets of five. [#rvm]_

John Paul II suggested praying the Luminous Mysteries on Thursdays, [#rvm38]_
and named October 2002 to October 2003 the Year of the Rosary. [#rvm3]_


The Rosary today
================

The Rosary is still one of the best-loved Catholic prayers. People pray it
alone, in families, in schools and in parishes. It can be prayed anywhere,
needs nothing but a string of beads (or ten fingers), and leads the person
praying through the whole Gospel story, from the Annunciation to the glory of
heaven.
