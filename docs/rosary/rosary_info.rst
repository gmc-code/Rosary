=========================
Rosary: user guide
=========================

The rosary tool shows the mysteries of the Rosary. Each mystery has its own
picture, its *fruit* (the grace prayed for) and ten scripture passages, one to
think about during each Hail Mary. The opening and closing prayers are
included, so a whole set can be prayed from start to finish, on screen or
read aloud.


Choosing the mysteries
======================

* **Tabs.** On a page with all four sets, click a tab to change set. The set
  for today has a gold *Today* label, and the page opens on it.
* **Mystery picker.** Under the set's heading, click one of the five
  pictures to choose a mystery. Under each name is its virtue, the *fruit of
  the mystery* (e.g. *Humility*), so the whole set's virtues can be seen at
  a glance. Untick **Show virtues** (under **Options**) to hide them. **Opening prayers** and **Closing prayers**
  sit at each end of the row when they are switched on.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 75

   * - Day
     - Mysteries
   * - Monday, Saturday
     - Joyful
   * - Tuesday, Friday
     - Sorrowful
   * - Wednesday, Sunday
     - Glorious
   * - Thursday
     - Luminous


Praying a decade with the beads
===============================

Under each mystery is a decade of beads:

* the **gold bead** is the Our Father. The mystery is announced here.
* beads **1 to 10** are the Hail Marys. Each bead shows its scripture passage
  above the list and highlights it in the list.
* the **Glory Be** bead ends the decade.

Click **Begin**, then **Next** for each prayer, or click any bead to jump to
it. After the Glory Be, **Finish decade** moves on to the next mystery of the
set, so all five decades can be prayed in order. **◀** (Back) goes back one
bead, and **⛶** opens *Full screen*. The 📖 button beside them drops down a
panel of tick boxes for what is shown under the beads (see *Meditations,
offerings and reflections*).

With **The ten steps** chosen under the beads, click a step in the list to go
to its bead.

Tick **Show the prayers** (under **Options**) to see the words of the Our
Father, Hail Mary and Glory Be (and the Fatima prayer, if it is on) on each
bead. This is useful when leading a class.


Opening and closing prayers
===========================

**Opening & closing** (under **Options**) adds the rest of the Rosary's
prayers, so a whole set can be prayed from start to finish:

* **Opening prayers**, before the first mystery: the Sign of the Cross, the
  Apostles' Creed, the Our Father for the intentions of the Holy Father, three
  Hail Marys for an increase in faith, hope and charity, and the Glory Be.
* **Closing prayers**, after the fifth mystery: the Hail, Holy Queen, "Pray
  for us, O holy Mother of God" (the versicle and response), the Rosary Prayer
  ("Let us pray. O God, whose only-begotten Son...") and the Sign of the
  Cross.
* **On, with St Michael and Memorare** also adds the Prayer to St Michael
  and the Memorare to the closing prayers.

They appear as **Opening prayers** and **Closing prayers** at each end of the
mystery picker, each with its own beads and the words of every prayer.
🔊 **All** on the opening prayers runs straight through: opening prayers, the five
mysteries, then the closing prayers.

Reading aloud reads only the words of each opening and closing
prayer. Tick **Read prayer names** (under **Options**) to have each one
announced first, with its intention, e.g. "Hail Mary, for an increase in
faith".

**Fatima prayer** (under **Options**, ticked to start with) adds "O my
Jesus, forgive us our sins..." after the Glory Be at the end of each decade.
It is read aloud with the Glory Be, and shown when **Show the prayers** is
ticked. Untick it to leave it out.


Starting choices
================

The first time someone opens the site, it starts on **Stained glass
windows** pictures (the mystery picker matching them), nothing under the
beads, the
**Automatic** layout (which picks Desktop, iPad or iPhone to fit the
screen), with pictures shown, **Current step, in the panel** verses and **Step
only** read aloud, with the opening and closing prayers and the Fatima
prayer on. After that, the
page remembers each reader's own choices from the **Options** menus.
**Reset to starting choices**, at the bottom of **Options**, forgets them
and goes back to these starting choices.


Choosing the pictures
=====================

Use **Pictures** (under **Options**) to change the picture shown for each
mystery. The page remembers the choice. The menu lists the choices in this
order.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Pictures
     - Look
   * - Stained glass windows (the starting choice)
     - Photographs of real stained-glass windows, flattened to face the front
   * - Cubism
     - Each mystery in the style of Cubism: bold, broken planes of colour
       and strong outlines. The mystery pictures are wide, so the iPad layout
       frames them to their shape
   * - Renaissance style
     - Newly painted pictures in the style of traditional Catholic sacred
       art, with Mary, Jesus and Joseph looking the same in every mystery
   * - Masterpieces
     - A famous painting of each mystery, such as Fra Angelico's
       *Annunciation* or Raphael's *Transfiguration*, with the artist and
       title underneath. Click a painting to see all of it (see
       :doc:`masterpieces </rosary/rosary_artworks>`)
   * - Gold icon
     - Figures on gold leaf, in the style of a Byzantine icon
   * - Stained glass (drawn)
     - An arched window with dark leading, jewel colours and a rose-window
       background
   * - Classic
     - Soft colours in a circle, in each set's colour

The opening and closing prayers have their own pictures too: in the
Masterpieces, Masaccio's *Holy Trinity* and Piero della Francesca's *Madonna
della Misericordia*. In the other picture sets, the opening picture is the
crucifix with a whole rosary (where the rosary begins) and the closing
picture is Mary as Queen and Mother of Mercy, sheltering her people under her
mantle (the Hail, Holy Queen). The drawn styles show a whole rosary, the loop of beads with the medal, the three Hail Marys,
the Our Father bead and the crucifix the opening prayers are said on
(opening), and hands joined in prayer with a rosary hanging across them
(closing). On the Sign of the Cross bead, the panel also says how to make
it: forehead, breast, left shoulder, then right shoulder.


Layout and full screen
======================

The top row holds the four sets (hover over one to see its days), then:

* **Options** opens the options panel, and closes it again. The panel has
  three groups, side by side on a wide screen and one above another on a
  phone:

  * **Pictures:** **Pictures**, **Mystery picker**, **Layout**, **Show
    pictures** and **Show virtues**.
  * **Prayers:** **Opening & closing**, **Under the beads**, **Scripture
    verses**, **Show the prayers** and **Fatima prayer**.
  * **Reading aloud:** **Voice**, **Read aloud**, **Speed** and **Read prayer
    names**.

  At the bottom, **Reset to starting choices** forgets the choices saved in
  this browser, and **Done** closes the panel.
* **Hide menus** hides the top row, the mystery picker, the page title and
  the site's header bar, leaving just the picture and its details. **Menus**
  brings them back.

**Mystery picker** (under **Options**) sets the small pictures in the row of
mysteries. *Same as pictures* matches the main pictures; choose another style
to mix them, e.g. simple **Classic** icons in the picker with **Stained glass
windows** as the main pictures. *Same as pictures* is the starting choice.

**Layout** (under **Options**) arranges each mystery for the size of
screen:

* **Automatic (fits the screen)** (the starting choice): chooses iPhone on a
  narrow screen, iPad on a medium one and Desktop on a wide one, and changes
  if the window is resized or a tablet is turned.
* **Desktop**: for a computer or large screen. The picture is as large as the
  screen allows on the left, shown whole, with the title, beads, buttons,
  current passage and the ten steps in a column on the right. On a wide
  screen the rosary also spreads into the empty space beside the page.
* **iPad**: the picture on the left, about the size of a tablet, with the
  title, beads and the ten steps beside it. The picture stays in view while
  the steps are scrolled.
* **iPhone**: built to fit a phone, with the page margins kept to a
  minimum:

  * The four sets fit on one line as *Joyful*, *Luminous*, *Sorrowful* and
    *Glorious*; a gold dot marks today's set.
  * Under them, **‹** and **›** move to the previous or next mystery
    (including the opening and closing prayers), carrying on into the next
    set at the end of one. They stay beside **Menus** when the menus are
    hidden.
  * The picture runs across the top, as large as fits, then the title.
  * The beads, from the Our Father to the Glory Be, fit on one line.
  * The buttons fit on one line too: **◀** (Back), **Next**, the 📖
    button, ⛶ (Full screen) and 🔊 **Bead** | **All**. In Full screen the 📖 button is left out to
    make room.
  * The mystery picker becomes one row that scrolls sideways.

On a narrow screen, Desktop and iPad put the details under the picture.

Untick **Show pictures** (under **Options**) to hide the main picture of
each mystery in any layout, so the text has the whole width. The small
pictures in the mystery picker stay. In **Full screen** the prayer then
shows large and centred.

**Scripture verses** (under **Options**) sets where the scripture passages
are shown:

* **All, under each step**: every step's passage, under its line in the
  list.
* **Current step, in the panel**: the list shows only the one-line summaries;
  the passage for the bead being prayed appears in the panel at the top, even
  when it isn't read aloud, and before the Hail Mary when **Show the
  prayers** is ticked. The opening and closing prayers work the same way:
  the words of the prayer being said appear in the panel.
* **Hidden**: no passages, only the summaries.

This only changes what is shown. What is read aloud is set by **Read aloud**
(see *Listening*).

**Full screen** (the **⛶** button under each mystery) fills the screen with
the current mystery, for projecting to a class. The picture takes up
three-quarters of the screen, with the title, beads and current passage
beside it (below it on a tall, narrow screen).

* The **right arrow**, **space bar** or **Enter** moves to the next bead;
  the **left arrow** goes back. A presentation clicker that sends Page Down
  and Page Up works too.
* After the Glory Be it carries on to the next mystery of the set.
* **Esc** or the **Exit** button (top right) leaves full screen.
* On a phone or tablet, swipe left for the next bead and right to go back.


iPhone and iPad
===============

Safari on iPhone does not let web pages go truly full screen, so **Full
screen** fills the Safari window instead, with an **Exit** button. For a real
full-screen rosary with no Safari bars:

#. Open the site in **Safari**.
#. Tap the **Share** button, then **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.
#. Open **Rosary** from the Home Screen. It opens full screen, like an app,
   with the rosary as its icon.

Use **Hide menus** as well to remove the page title and menus.


Meditations, offerings and reflections
======================================

**Under the beads** (under **Options**) has a tick box for each thing a
mystery can show under its beads. Tick any mix of them; they appear in this
order:

* **The ten steps**: the ten scripture references, one for each Hail Mary.
* **A meditation**: a short meditation on the mystery, about a hundred words,
  ending with a one-line prayer. Each one draws on the mystery's scripture
  and leads to its fruit.
* **A meditation on the virtue**: about 80 words on the mystery's fruit:
  what the virtue is, how the mystery shows it, and how to practise it,
  ending with a short prayer for it. It is headed with the virtue, e.g.
  *The virtue: Humility*.
* **An intention**: who and what the decade is offered for, linked to the
  mystery, e.g. the Annunciation is offered for expectant mothers, unborn
  children and all discerning God's call, and the Carrying of the Cross for
  the sick, carers and all who carry heavy burdens. A short reflection and
  prayer follow. The last Glorious Mystery is left for the reader's own
  intentions.
* **A Montfort-style offering**: a short prayer offering the decade, in the
  manner of St Louis de Montfort's *The Secret of the Rosary*. It offers the
  decade to Jesus in honour of the mystery and asks, through Mary's prayers,
  for the mystery's fruit, e.g. "We offer you, Lord Jesus, this first decade
  in honour of your Incarnation... we ask of you a deep humility of heart."
* **A classic meditation**: the meditations from *The Rosary*, a
  Dominican booklet printed in San Francisco in 1915, now in the public
  domain. Each has a title (the virtue it teaches, e.g. *Mary's Humility*), a
  reflection of about 80 to 150 words in the language of its day, and an
  invocation to Mary. The book has the fifteen traditional mysteries only, so
  the Luminous Mysteries, added in 2002, show the meditation instead, with a
  note saying so. Its virtues sometimes differ from the fruits used
  elsewhere on the site, e.g. *Zeal for Souls* for the Finding in the Temple.
* **A modern reflection**: about 70 words in plain, everyday English,
  connecting the mystery to ordinary life (being honest with God, turning up
  for people, standing up for someone who is being picked on), ending with a
  question to think about. These suit students well.

With nothing ticked (the starting choice), nothing is shown, for a clean page:
the current step and its passage still show in the panel at the top on each
bead. **All** ticks every meditation (all but the steps), for reading or
comparing them, and **None** unticks everything.

To change them quickly, click the 📖 button beside **Next** in any mystery.
It drops down the same tick boxes (**Steps**, **Meditation**, **Virtue**,
**Intention**, **Offering**, **Classic (1915)** and **Modern**, with **All**
and **None**). Click anywhere else, or press Esc, to close it. The choice
applies to every mystery, and **Under the beads** in **Options** changes to
match.

The beads, buttons and the panel at the top work the same whichever is
chosen. The Listen buttons read whatever meditations are ticked on the Our
Father bead, after announcing the mystery, each introduced by its heading when
more than one is ticked, and in **Full screen** they appear in the panel
beside the picture on that bead (scroll the panel for the rest). Bible
references in brackets in the classic meditations are left out when they are
read aloud. Ticking many makes the Our Father bead long to listen to.
The opening and closing prayers still show their list of prayers.

The meditations, offerings and reflections were written for this site, so
they can be used and edited freely; the classic meditations are in the
public domain.


Reading the passages
====================

The passages are from the **Catholic Public Domain Version** (CPDV), a
modern English translation of the Latin Vulgate. Where they appear is set by
**Scripture verses** (see *Layout and full screen*): under each step, in the
panel at the top for the current bead only, or not at all.

* The short line after each reference is a summary of the passage.
* Each reference is also a link to the passage on BibleGateway (NRSV Catholic
  Edition), for comparing translations.
* The CPDV follows the Vulgate's numbering, so a few passages have a different
  number there, shown in brackets, e.g. Psalm 16:10 is *(CPDV Psalm 15:10)*.


Listening
=========

The rosary can be read aloud by the computer's own voice. Nothing has to be
downloaded, but sound must be on.

The 🔊 Listen control under the beads, 🔊 **Bead** | **All**, has two buttons
(the same in every layout):

* **Bead** reads the current bead once: the mystery on the Our Father bead,
  or the step (and its passage, if chosen) on a Hail Mary bead. On the
  opening and closing prayers it is called **Prayer** and reads the current
  prayer.
* **All** prays aloud bead by bead, moving on by itself, then carries on to
  the next mystery of the set. On the opening and closing prayers it reads
  all the prayers in turn.

While it is reading, the button becomes **■ Stop**; click it to stop.
Clicking a bead while it is praying aloud carries on from that bead.

* **Read aloud** (under **Options**) sets what is read on each Hail Mary
  bead: **Step only** (the one-line summary), **Step and scripture**, or
  **Scripture only**.
* Tick **Show the prayers** (under **Options**) to have the Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be and
  O my Jesus read aloud too. Leave it unticked to hear only the scripture, as a
  quiet meditation.
* Choose the **Voice** and **Speed** under **Options** (in *Reading aloud*).
  The page picks an Australian voice if the computer has one, and remembers
  your choice.

Voices differ between computers. On Windows, the Microsoft Edge browser has
the most natural voices (those marked *Natural* or *Online*). Chrome on any
computer has the Google voices.


Printing
========

Printing the page prints every mystery shown on it, with its picture, fruit,
ten references and their passages, in the picture style chosen, always in a
plain layout with a small picture. The opening and closing prayers print too, with their words,
when they are switched on. The tabs, beads, buttons and options are left out.
On the *All the mysteries* page, all four sets print. Choose **Hidden** in
**Scripture verses** first to print the references without the passages.
