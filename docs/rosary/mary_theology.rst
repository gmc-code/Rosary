=============================
A short theology of Mary
=============================

Catholics give Mary, the mother of Jesus, a special place in their faith and
prayer. But everything the Church says about Mary is really about Jesus.
What we believe about her comes from what we believe about him, and helps us
understand him better. [#ccc-p1k]_


In brief
========

- **Mother of God.** Mary's son is God the Son, so she is truly the Mother
  of God.
- **Full of grace.** God kept her free from sin from the first moment of her
  life: the *Immaculate Conception*.
- **Ever-virgin.** Mary was a virgin when she conceived Jesus, and remained
  one all her life.
- **Taken up to heaven.** At the end of her life she was taken, body and
  soul, into heaven: the *Assumption*.
- **Our mother.** Jesus gave her to us as our mother, and she prays for us.
- **The first disciple.** Her "yes" to God makes her a model of faith for
  every Christian.


Mother of God
=============

When Mary visited her cousin Elizabeth, Elizabeth greeted her as "the mother
of my Lord" (Luke 1:43). The child Mary carried was not only a man; he was
the eternal Son of God, the second person of the Trinity. So the Church calls
Mary *Theotokos*, Greek for "God-bearer" or "Mother of God". The Council of
Ephesus confirmed this title in 431. [#ccc-p1k]_

This title does not mean that Mary existed before God or made God. It says
something about Jesus: he is truly God and truly man, one person, and Mary is
his mother.


Full of grace: the Immaculate Conception
========================================

The angel Gabriel greeted Mary as "full of grace" (Luke 1:28). The Church
teaches that, from the very first moment of her life in her mother's womb,
God kept Mary free from original sin. This is the *Immaculate Conception*.
It was a gift, given because of what her Son would do: Jesus saved Mary too,
but in an even greater way. [#ccc-p1k]_

Pope Pius IX formally defined this teaching on 8 December 1854, the feast of
the Immaculate Conception. [#ineffabilis]_

A common mix-up: the Immaculate Conception is about how *Mary* began her
life, free from sin. It is not about Jesus being conceived without a human
father. That is the *virgin birth*.


Ever-virgin
===========

The Gospels tell us that Mary conceived Jesus by the power of the Holy
Spirit, without a human father (Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 1:34–35). The Church
also teaches that Mary remained a virgin all her life, and calls her
"ever-virgin". [#ccc-p1k]_

Her virginity shows that Jesus is a gift from God, not something people
achieved on their own. It also shows how completely Mary gave herself to God.
[#ccc-p1k]_


Taken up to heaven: the Assumption
==================================

The Church teaches that when Mary's life on earth was over, God took her,
body and soul, into the glory of heaven. This is the *Assumption*. What
happened to Mary is a sign of what Christians hope for: that one day we too
will share, body and soul, in Jesus' Resurrection. [#ccc-p2c]_

Pope Pius XII formally defined this teaching on 1 November 1950. It is
celebrated on 15 August. [#munificentissimus]_


Mother of the Church
====================

As he was dying on the cross, Jesus saw his mother and the disciple he
loved standing nearby. He said to her, "Woman, here is your son," and to the
disciple, "Here is your mother" (John 19:26–27). Christians have always seen
in this moment Jesus giving Mary to all his followers as their mother.
[#lg]_

So the Church calls Mary "Mother of the Church". From heaven she continues to
care for us and pray for us, as any mother would. [#ccc-p2c]_


The first disciple
==================

When the angel told Mary she would be the mother of Jesus, she answered,
"Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your
word" (Luke 1:38). She trusted God before she understood everything.

Mary followed Jesus all the way, from Bethlehem to the cross, and prayed with
the apostles as they waited for the Holy Spirit (Acts 1:14). That is why the
Church sees her as a model of faith and love for every Christian. [#lg]_
[#ccc-p2c]_


Honour, not worship
===================

Catholics do not worship Mary. Worship belongs to God alone. Instead, the
Church *honours* Mary in a special way, because of who she is and what God
did in her. This honour always leads back to God. [#ccc-p2c]_

When Catholics pray to Mary, they are asking her to pray for them, much as
we might ask a friend to pray for us. The Hail Mary ends this way: "Holy
Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death."


Mary and the Rosary
===================

The Rosary is the best-known way Catholics pray with Mary. It brings together
much of what is on this page: the Hail Mary, the events of Mary's life with
Jesus, and the Glorious Mysteries of her Assumption and crowning in heaven.
As we pray it, Mary leads us to her Son.

You can pray :doc:`all the mysteries </rosary/rosary>`, read about
:doc:`the theology of the Rosary </rosary/rosary_theology>`, or pray other
:doc:`Marian prayers </rosary/marian_prayers>`.
