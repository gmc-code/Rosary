===========================
The theology of the Rosary
===========================

The Rosary is a prayer *with* Mary, but it is really *about* Jesus. As we say
the prayers, we think about the great moments of his life, death and rising.
Mary prays alongside us, as the one who knew him best. Pope St John Paul II
put it this way: the Rosary is Marian in character, but "at heart a
Christocentric prayer", that is, a prayer centred on Christ. [#rvm1]_


In brief
========

- **It is about Jesus.** Each mystery is an event in his life.
- **Mary shows us how to look.** She remembered and thought deeply about all
  that her Son said and did.
- **The words and the mysteries go together.** The Hail Marys keep us praying
  while we think about each mystery.
- **It shapes how we live.** Thinking about Jesus' life helps us grow more
  like him.


A Gospel prayer
===============

The mysteries take us through the main events of the Gospels: Jesus' birth
and childhood, his public life, his suffering and death, his Resurrection and
Ascension, and the coming of the Holy Spirit. Pope St Paul VI called the
Rosary a "Gospel prayer", centred on how God became man and saved us.
[#mc46]_

The Rosary is sometimes called a *compendium*, or summary, of the Gospel. It
does not cover every part of Jesus' life, and it does not replace reading the
Gospels. Instead, it gathers their central events so that we can keep coming
back to them and think about them more deeply. [#rvm1]_ [#rvm29]_


Looking at Jesus with Mary
==========================

St Luke tells us that Mary "treasured all these things in her heart"
(Luke 2:51). John Paul II saw her as a model for praying the Rosary: when we
pray it, we remember Jesus the way she did, with love and attention.
[#rvm11]_

Mary is also a teacher. No one knows Jesus better than his mother. At the
wedding at Cana she tells the servants, "Do whatever he tells you"
(John 2:5). In the Rosary she points us to her Son in the same way, and
teaches us to listen to him. [#rvm14]_

So Mary's place in the Rosary is to lead us to Jesus, not to take his place.
[#rvm11]_


Words and mysteries together
============================

The Hail Mary is built from words in Luke's Gospel: the angel's greeting to
Mary, and Elizabeth's blessing on "the fruit of your womb", Jesus. So every
Hail Mary points to the same Jesus we see in the mystery. Paul VI described
the run of Hail Marys as the thread on which our thinking about the
mysteries is woven. [#mc46]_

The words and the scenes belong together. We are not just saying words, and
we are not just picturing scenes: each helps the other. [#mc46]_ [#rvm24]_

The Luminous Mysteries, added by John Paul II in 2002, bring in Jesus' public
life, from his Baptism to the Last Supper. With them, the mysteries cover the
Gospel story more fully. [#rvm19]_


Why so much repetition?
=======================

The Rosary joins spoken prayer to quiet thinking. The repeated prayers give a
steady rhythm, like a heartbeat. Meanwhile, each announced mystery gives the
mind and imagination a scene from Jesus' life to stay with. The rhythm is
there to help us pay attention, not to do the praying for us. [#rvm12]_
[#rvm29]_

John Paul II quotes this warning from Paul VI:

   "Without contemplation, the Rosary is a body without a soul, and its
   recitation runs the risk of becoming a mechanical repetition of formulas."
   [#rvm12]_

So the Rosary is best prayed slowly, with time to think. The prayers keep the
mystery in front of us, and the thinking lets its meaning sink in. [#rvm12]_


A prayer that shapes our lives
==============================

Thinking about Jesus' life is more than remembering the past. John Paul II
taught that when we prayerfully remember Jesus' life, death and Resurrection,
we open ourselves to the grace he won for us. Little by little, the mysteries
shape the way we live. [#rvm13]_

The Rosary does not replace the Mass and the Church's other liturgy, or
reading the Bible. John Paul II saw it as a prayer that sits alongside the
liturgy and leads us more deeply into the Gospel. [#rvm13]_ [#rvm29]_

In the end, the Rosary is both simple and deep: the repeated words keep us
attentive, Mary teaches us to look at her Son, and the mysteries help us come
to know Jesus better.
